# Aangan — where the product should go

Written from a competitive/startup lens, against MyGate and NoBrokerHood.
Opinionated on purpose. Status: discussion, nothing here is built.

---

## 1. The uncomfortable starting point

**Aangan already has more community surface than MyGate and NoBrokerHood
combined.** Feed, directory, DMs, marketplace, home food, tiffins, borrow &
lend, lost & found, recommendations, sports with dues, documents, payments,
polls, properties, nearby places, blood donors, emergency, society functions
with published accounts — and an AI agent over all of it.

So the honest conclusion is: **more features will not win a society.** If
feature count decided this, Aangan would already have won.

The bottleneck is not the product. It is *getting installed*.

---

## 2. Why the incumbents win, precisely

MyGate and NoBrokerHood won on **the gate**, and the gate has three properties
nothing in Aangan currently has:

1. **It is bought by the committee, not chosen by residents.** One decision
   installs it on 300 phones.
2. **It is mandatory.** You cannot receive a delivery without it.
3. **It has an ops moat** — guard tablets, guard training, gate hardware,
   support. Expensive and boring, which is exactly what makes it defensible.

The result: they are **utilities**. Opened when a delivery arrives, closed
immediately. Their community features are widely regarded as poor — a noisy
feed, a thin marketplace, and vendor lead-gen that residents actively resent.

**You cannot out-gate them**, and you already decided not to try. That's right.
Guards, tablets and gate hardware would consume everything and you would arrive
late to a solved problem.

**But you don't have to.** The real market fact is the one you stated yourself:
*"we have NoBrokerHood for visitors."* Societies run both. The gate app and the
community app are different jobs, and the incumbents are only good at one.

**Strategy: be the life of the society, not the security of it. Coexist first,
displace later.**

---

## 3. The two things Aangan genuinely owns

**Home food.** Nobody else has it. It is a *daily decision* with real money
attached, and it is the only feature here with natural transaction revenue.
This is the wedge — not a nice-to-have module.

**Saathi.** A real agent over society data, already able to search, count, find
residents, read polls, hold standing watches, and propose posts, listings,
orders, messages and polls. Neither incumbent has anything close, and neither
can ship it quickly — they are large companies with a security product and a
different centre of gravity.

Everything below is in service of those two.

---

## 4. The bets

### A. Manufacture one daily habit

Nothing retains without a reason to open the app on an ordinary Tuesday.

1. **The 11am menu push.** *"3 kitchens cooking today — Rajma chawal, Bisi bele
   bath, Egg curry."* One notification, timed to the lunch decision. This is the
   highest-leverage retention change available and it uses only what exists.
2. **Standing orders.** Tiffin exists; make it *"every Tue & Thu, automatically"*.
   Recurring behaviour beats recurring reminders.
3. **Group orders.** Ten flats order from one kitchen at once. The cook gets
   scale, residents get a lower price, and it creates a scheduled moment the
   whole society participates in.

### B. Remove the adoption friction — the actual bottleneck

4. **A WhatsApp bridge.** Indian societies live in WhatsApp groups, and they are
   not leaving. A daily digest posted into the group — *"Today on Aangan: 3
   dishes, a cycle for sale, 2 flats hunting for a plumber"* — with deep links,
   meets residents where they already are and turns the group into a
   distribution channel instead of a competitor. **Probably the single highest-ROI
   thing on this list.**
5. **Zero-committee onboarding.** One resident should be able to start their
   society and invite neighbours, with verification by neighbours vouching
   rather than committee approval. MyGate structurally cannot grow this way —
   it needs the committee. This is the growth motion they can't copy.

### C. Make Saathi indispensable

6. **Society memory.** *"What did we decide about parking?"* *"Who's the plumber
   everyone recommends?"* *"When was the water tank last cleaned?"* — answered
   from feed history, polls, documents and past functions. This compounds: every
   month of use makes it better, and it cannot be replicated without the data.
   The strongest long-term moat in this document.
7. **Voice, in the local language.** Hindi and Kannada voice in, spoken answer
   out. A large share of residents — particularly older ones — will never type a
   query in English. Translation infrastructure already exists here. Low
   competition, very high delight.
8. **The notice-board camera.** Photograph the paper notice taped inside the
   lift → Saathi turns it into a structured announcement and pushes it to
   everyone. Ten seconds, uses vision that already exists, and it is the single
   most demoable magic moment available. It also digitises the *actual* current
   system, which is paper.

### D. Money is what makes it sticky

9. **Split anything.** The function-accounts ledger already does contributions,
   expenses and a published report. Generalise it: an airport cab, a bulk
   vegetable order, a shared cook, a water tanker. Shared money creates
   obligation, and obligation creates retention.
10. **A trusted vendor list with real ratings.** *"The society's own Justdial"* —
    the plumber 40 flats actually used, with real reviews. This is a compounding
    community asset. MyGate monetises vendor leads in a way residents dislike;
    doing it honestly is both a better product and a defensible position.

---

## 5. What NOT to build

- **Visitor / gate management.** Hardware, guards, training, support. You lose,
  slowly and expensively.
- **Committee accounting / maintenance ERP.** Long sales cycles, entrenched
  incumbents, and it drags you into the top-down motion you're avoiding.
- **Anything that needs committee sign-off before a resident sees value.** That
  is the incumbents' game and their home turf.

---

## 6. On money

MyGate monetises via ads and vendor lead-gen, which residents resent — that
resentment is an opening.

Aangan has one feature with honest, aligned transaction revenue: **home food**.
A small cut of order value grows only when residents genuinely benefit. That is
a far better place to start than advertising, and it argues for pushing hard on
§4A before anything else.

A per-society subscription is the obvious second line, but it should follow
adoption, not gate it.

---

## 7. If I had to pick three

In order:

1. **The 11am menu push** — cheapest to build, largest effect on retention.
2. **The WhatsApp bridge** — solves distribution, which is the real problem.
3. **Saathi voice + the notice-board camera** — the demo that makes a society
   secretary say *"wait, do that again."*

Everything else in this document is incremental. These three change the
trajectory.

---

## 8. The thing worth being honest about

None of this is a technology problem, and Aangan's engineering is well ahead of
its distribution. The hard question is not *"what should we build?"* — it is
*"how does society #2 hear about this?"*

Society #1 is running because you built it. Nothing above matters until there
is a repeatable answer for #2, and that answer is much more likely to be §4B
than any feature in §4A, C or D.
